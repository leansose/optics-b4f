3- Service: When Do We Need to Increase Operational Capacity?
    Order Volume Throughout the Year =>
        During November and December:
            - 2024: 6,497 orders received.
            - 2025: 6,483 orders received.
    That's approximately 106 orders per day in both years.
    In July and August, the average was around 50 orders per day.
    Conclusion: Order volume at the end of the year is almost more than twice the summer period.
    This means the company needs to prepare greater production and operational capacity during November and December.

4- Order Delivery Delays

Out of a total of **49,343 delivered orders:**

| Delivery Status        | Number of Orders |
| ---------------------- | ---------------- |
| Delivered on time      | 20,898           |
| Delayed by 1 day       | 20,979           |
| Delayed by 2 to 9 days | 7,466            |

Orders delayed by 2 to 9 days represent **15.1% of total deliveries**, but account for **66.1% of all delay days**.
This makes them the most important group to investigate.

**There is a data issue that prevents us from accurately identifying the source of delays:**

- All production records end either on the expected delivery date, one day before it, or two days before it.
- The logistics record date always matches the order creation date.

**The data does not include:**

- The actual date the order was shipped.
- The date the carrier received the order.

Therefore, we cannot conclusively determine whether the delays are caused by production or transportation.
as a solution: Add the shipping date and the date the carrier receives the order.

** Problems and Solutions
## **Main Problems and Proposed Solutions**

| Problem                                                                                                                                    | Proposed Solution                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Unclear Production Costs:** It is not clear whether `custo_real` represents the total cost per order or the cost per individual lens. | Confirm with the production team and then adopt the correct calculation method.         |
| **2. High Problem Rates in Models L001–L012:** They account for 33.6% of orders but are responsible for 80.8% of the total problem costs.  | Review the manufacturing process and quality of these models, starting with model L001. |
| **3. Low-Priced Orders and Negative Profitability:** 923 delivered orders had a negative contribution.                                     | Review discounts and invoices and verify that prices are correct.                       |
| **4. Delivery Delays:** 15.1% of delivered orders were delayed by two or more days.                                                        | Record shipping dates and carrier receipt dates to identify the source of delays.       |
| **5. Problems Not Linked to Orders:** 185 issues, worth €29,736.69, have no matching order.                                                | Review the codes and try to link each issue to the correct order.                       |
| **6. Duplicate Orders:** There are 1,027 duplicate records.                                                                                | Remove exact duplicates before analysis to avoid inflating the numbers.                 |
| **7. Inaccurate Customer Satisfaction Data:** Some ratings are not linked to orders or were recorded before delivery.                      | Link ratings to the correct orders and only consider ratings submitted after delivery.  |
| **8. Differences in Optical Store Performance:** Some stores handle more complex models than others.                                       | Compare store performance after adjusting the results for model type and complexity.    |

** Based on the report's findings, we should focus on four key actions: **

1. **Production:** Review production costs and the quality of models L001–L012.
2. **Financial:** Confirm the method used to calculate production costs and review orders with negative contributions.
3. **Logistics:** Add shipping dates and carrier receipt dates to identify the causes of delays.
4. **Data Quality:** Fix mismatched codes, duplicate records, and missing data.
